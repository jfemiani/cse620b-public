Agricultural Remote Sensing

Multispectral imagery records reflected energy in several wavelength bands. Reflectance is the fraction of incoming light returned by a surface. Green leaves absorb much of the red light used in photosynthesis and reflect near-infrared (NIR) energy. The canopy is the leaves and branches seen from above. Repeated observations can show how vegetation changes through a growing season. A vegetation index combines bands into a compact signal; the normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI), for example, contrasts NIR and red reflectance: (NIR - red) / (NIR + red). An index is a clue about canopy conditions, not a direct measurement of crop type, yield, or cause of stress.

Learning goals

  • Explain why repeated observations are useful for monitoring crop development.
  • Calculate the share of labeled pixels belonging to selected crop classes.
  • Distinguish a reference land-cover label from a measurement of crop health.
