Sentinel-2 Level-1C at Harvard Forest¶

A worked look at a real Sentinel-2A multispectral image over Harvard Forest, Massachusetts. The scene was acquired on June 11, 2023, in tile T18TYN. We read a compact, georeferenced chip around the Harvard Forest EMS tower, decode its stored digital numbers (DN) with the product's radiometric metadata, and plot all 13 bands, including the atmospheric bands.

This notebook is a demonstration, not an exercise. The prepared chip keeps the lesson download small; all imagery comes from the public Level-1C product.

Acquisition and provenance¶

  • Site: Harvard Forest EMS tower, approximately 42.5378° N, 72.1715° W.
  • Acquisition: Sentinel-2A MSI, 2023-06-11 15:51:19 UTC.
  • Product: S2A_MSIL1C_20230611T153821_N0509_R011_T18TYN_20230611T205742.SAFE
  • Tile: T18TYN, UTM zone 18N (EPSG:32618); product cloud-cover estimate is about 5%.

The source JP2 bands and tile metadata are available anonymously from the AWS Open Data Sentinel-2 L1C bucket. For example, B04 (red) is a 10 m JPEG2000 image. The product XML metadata is the source of the quantification value and band offsets used below. The acquisition is also indexed as S2A_18TYN_20230611_0_L1C.

Full 10 m JP2 files are roughly 100 MB each. To make this notebook practical to run in Colab, the lesson uses GeoTIFF crops around the same 2 km square. The original six-band chip averages the 10 m bands onto a 20 m grid. The additional bands are separate GeoTIFFs at their native 20 m or 60 m spacing. The 60 m crops extend slightly past the display boundary so their native pixels cover the whole square.

Level-1C, Level-2A, and reflectance¶

Level-1C is orthorectified top-of-atmosphere (TOA) reflectance: it has not had the atmosphere removed. Level-2A applies atmospheric correction to estimate bottom-of-atmosphere (BOA, or surface) reflectance and includes a scene-classification layer. This sample is Level-1C, so do not interpret its values as surface reflectance or expect an L2A cloud mask.

Sentinel-2 stores reflectance as integer DN. The product XML reports QUANTIFICATION_VALUE = 10000, RADIO_ADD_OFFSET = -1000 for each band, NODATA = 0, and SATURATED = 65535. Thus, for valid pixels:

[ \rho_{\mathrm{TOA}} = \frac{\mathrm{DN} + \mathrm{RADIO_ADD_OFFSET}} {\mathrm{QUANTIFICATION_VALUE}} = \frac{\mathrm{DN} - 1000}{10000}. ]

Read those values from the actual product metadata rather than assuming every Sentinel-2 processing baseline uses the same offset.

Bands in this chip¶

Wavelengths are approximate band-center wavelengths. The 10 m channels have been averaged over aligned 2 × 2 blocks for the shared 20 m chip grid.

Band Spectral region Approx. center Native resolution What it shows
B01 Coastal aerosol 443 nm 60 m Sensitive to atmospheric scattering; useful for aerosol correction and coastal water.
B02 Blue 490 nm 10 m The blue channel in natural color; sensitive to haze and useful over shallow water.
B03 Green 560 nm 10 m Captures the visible green reflectance peak of many plants.
B04 Red 665 nm 10 m Chlorophyll absorbs strongly here; this is the red band in NDVI.

B06 Red edge 2 740 nm 20 m Samples farther up that rise; helps characterize canopy chlorophyll.

B08 Broad near-infrared 833 nm 10 m Healthy vegetation often reflects strongly here; this is the NIR band in NDVI.

B09 Water vapor 945 nm 60 m Samples a water-vapor absorption feature; used to estimate atmospheric water vapor.
B10 Cirrus 1375 nm 60 m Water vapor suppresses much of the surface signal, so high cirrus can stand out. Level-2A omits this band.

B12 Shortwave infrared 2 2202 nm 20 m Helps distinguish burned areas, exposed materials, and moisture differences.

Read the chip and its product metadata¶

In [4]:
product_id = "S2A_MSIL1C_20230611T153821_N0509_R011_T18TYN_20230611T205742"
chip_name = "sentinel-2-l1c-harvard-forest-2023-06-11.tif"
data_dir = Path("sentinel-2-l1c-demo") / "data"
chip_path = data_dir / chip_name
product_metadata = ElementTree.parse(data_dir / "metadata.xml").getroot()

metadata_fields = {
    element.tag.rsplit("}", 1)[-1]: element
    for element in product_metadata.iter()
}
quantification = int(metadata_fields["QUANTIFICATION_VALUE"].text)
add_offsets = {
    element.attrib["band_id"]: int(element.text)
    for element in product_metadata.iter()
    if element.tag.rsplit("}", 1)[-1] == "RADIO_ADD_OFFSET"
}
special_values = {}
for element in product_metadata.iter():
    if element.tag.rsplit("}", 1)[-1] == "Special_Values":
        values = {child.tag.rsplit("}", 1)[-1]: child.text for child in element}
        special_values[values["SPECIAL_VALUE_TEXT"]] = int(values["SPECIAL_VALUE_INDEX"])

with rasterio.open(chip_path) as chip:
    dn = {
        band: chip.read(index).astype(np.float32)
        for index, band in enumerate(("B02", "B03", "B04", "B08", "B11", "B12"), start=1)
    }
    bounds = chip.bounds
    chip_crs = chip.crs
band_bounds = dict.fromkeys(dn, bounds)
for band in bands.keys() - dn.keys():
    with rasterio.open(data_dir / f"{band}.tif") as chip:
        dn[band] = chip.read(1).astype(np.float32)
        band_bounds[band] = chip.bounds

no_data = special_values["NODATA"]
saturated = special_values["SATURATED"]
toa = {
    band: np.where(
        (dn[band] != no_data) & (dn[band] != saturated),
        (dn[band] + add_offsets[bands[band]["id"]]) / quantification,
        np.nan,
    )
    for band in band_order
}

print(f"Product: {product_id}")
print(f"{len(toa)} bands; {chip_crs}")
for band, image in toa.items():
    print(f"{band}: {image.shape[0]} rows × {image.shape[1]} columns")
print(f"Metadata: quantification={quantification}, offsets={sorted(set(add_offsets.values()))}")
print(f"Special values: {special_values}")

Natural-color RGB¶

B04/B03/B02 are assigned to red/green/blue. The composite shows the visible landscape around the tower, marked with a yellow cross. A per-channel percentile stretch and gamma adjustment make it easier to see on screen; they are display operations, not additional reflectance corrections.

Obtaining a different source scene¶

To work with another date or location, use the Copernicus Browser: sign in with a Copernicus Data Space account, search for the area and acquisition date, select Sentinel-2 → Level-1C, and choose a scene with suitable cloud cover. Download the product (or the required tile and bands) as a SAFE product. Its MTD_MSIL1C.xml metadata supplies the quantification value, band-specific RADIO_ADD_OFFSET values, and special DN values. Keep the processing level and the matching metadata together; Level-2A uses BOA offsets and should not be decoded using the Level-1C formula above.

For a direct public band URL, such as the B04 link above, use wget -O B04.jp2 'BAND_URL'. This downloads the entire band file.
